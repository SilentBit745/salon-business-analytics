
# A/B Experiment Design

## 1. Objective
To check whether a new business plan improves customer retention.

## 2. Hypothesis

- H0: The new plan does not improve customer retention.
- H1: The new plan improves customer retention.

## 3. Experiment Setup

- Control Group: Customers using the existing plan.
- Treatment Group: Customers using the new plan.
- Randomization: Customers are divided randomly.
- Group Size: 50% Control and 50% Treatment.
- Follow-up: 30 days.

## 4. Metrics

**Primary Metric:**
- Customer retention rate.

**Guardrail Metrics:**
- Revenue per visit.
- No-show rate.

## 5. Sample Size

Assumptions:

- Existing retention: 41.56%.
- Expected retention: 46.56%.
- Significance level: 5%.
- Statistical power: 80%.

Required sample:

- Control: Approximately 1,547 customers.
- Treatment: Approximately 1,547 customers.
- Total: Approximately 3,094 customers.

## 6. Experiment Duration

- Recruitment time depends on customer availability.
- Each customer is observed for 30 days.
- Final results are checked after follow-up.

## 7. Data Checking

- Check equal group sizes.
- Check customer details.
- Check missing data.
- Check sample ratio mismatch (SRM).

## 8. Analysis

- Compare customer retention.
- Compare revenue per visit.
- Compare no-show rates.
- Calculate p-values and 95% confidence intervals.

## 9. Final Decision

- Go: Results show improvement without major harm.
- No-Go: Results show harm or no useful improvement.
- Extend: More data is needed.

## 10. Limitations

- The data is simulated.
- Actual business results may be different.
- Missing data may affect results.
- The experiment assumptions may not match real customers.
